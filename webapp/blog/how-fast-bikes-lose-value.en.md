"What is my bike still worth?" is the question we get most. The honest answer: mostly what it cost new and how old it is. This is how Bikecheck's [value estimator](/value) works it out, and how you can do it yourself.

## The curve

A road bike loses **about 25% in its first year** (a new bike is "used" after its first ride) and after that **about 12% a year** of what's left. For a bike that cost €5,000 new:

| Age | Share of new price | Value |
|---|---|---|
| This year | 80% | €4,000 |
| 1 year | 75% | €3,750 |
| 2 years | 66% | €3,300 |
| 3 years | 58% | €2,900 |
| 4 years | 51% | €2,560 |
| 5 years | 45% | €2,250 |
| 6 years | 40% | €1,980 |
| 8 years | 31% | €1,530 |
| 10 years | 24% | €1,190 |

![How the value estimator works it out: the line is the estimate, the band the fair price.](chart:value-curve)

Then condition: **like new** counts 5% higher, **used** 8% lower and **damaged** 40% lower. A bike ten years or older is never "like new", whatever the ad says: rubber, bearings and the frame have aged. If parts were replaced by a cheaper class, say a 105 groupset on a bike sold with Ultegra, another 10% comes off for the first step down.

Around the result we give a range of 10% either side. Anything in that range we call a fair price.

## The trap: which new price?

This is where it often goes wrong. A seller works from what they paid in the shop, a buyer looks at what the same bike costs now. Those two are far apart.

Our archive has **75 models** whose new price we know in two years at least three years apart. The median rise is **24%**, about 4.5% a year. A few examples:

| Model | Then | Now | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canyon Ultimate CF SLX 8 Di2 | €3,699 (2019) | €6,099 (2025) | +65% |
| Giant Propel Advanced 1 | €2,299 (2020) | €4,399 (2025) | +91% |
| Giant TCR Advanced 1 | €2,299 (2021) | €3,499 (2026) | +52% |
| BMC URS ONE | €8,999 (2020) | €4,499 (2026) | −50% |

![New prices from the catalogue and the archived spec sheets.](chart:price-rises)

Working from today's price makes an older bike too expensive. A 2021 TCR Advanced 1 cost €2,299 back then; five years on the curve puts it at about €1,030, not the €1,570 you get from today's new price. That's why, where we have it, Bikecheck uses **the new price of the bike's own model year**.

It works the other way too: some models, like the BMC URS above, got cheaper. Then an old bike is worth relatively more than the new price suggests.
