The groupset (shifters, derailleurs, crankset, brakes) is what brands build their price ladder on. The same frame often comes in four or five builds, and the only difference is the groupset. That makes it the quickest way to see which price class a bike in an ad belongs to.

We grouped the bikes in our [catalogue](/models) by their groupset and worked out the median new price for each.

## Three things to remember

**Electronic shifting costs about €1,400 more.** A bike with 105 Di2 has a median price of €3,999, with mechanical 105 €2,599. Ultegra shows a similar gap. Much of that is the bike around it: Di2 bikes often get a better frame or wheels too.

**SRAM sits higher than you'd think.** Rival costs about as much as Ultegra, and Force more than Ultegra Di2. That's because SRAM's road groupsets are now nearly all wireless electronic (AXS). So compare a SRAM bike with Shimano Di2, not with mechanical Shimano.

**The groupset says something about the rest of the bike.** Brands rarely pair a top groupset with an entry frame. If an ad says Dura-Ace, a light carbon frame and good wheels should come with it. If the frame is cheaper or the wheels have been swapped, ask: the groupset may have been fitted later.

## How to use this second-hand

A bike loses value every year, but the gaps between groupsets mostly stay. A five-year-old bike with Ultegra Di2 is still worth more than one with 105 of the same age, if frame and condition are similar.
