An ad says "size M" or "56 cm", and that's often all you get. The trouble is that sizes aren't a standard. Every brand draws its own lines, and sometimes they differ between models of the same brand. Before you drive an hour to see a bike, it's worth checking that the size in the ad is your size *for that model*.

We collected the size charts of **1,110 bikes** in our catalogue: for 350 the brand gives a rider height per size, for the rest we have the frame geometry.

## "M" isn't one size

Take a rider of 1.78 m:

| Bike | Size at 1.78 m | What that size covers |
|---|---|---|
| [Trek Domane SL 5 Gen 5](/models/trek-domane-sl-5-gen-5) | ML | 173 to 179 cm |
| [Canyon Endurace CF 7](/models/canyon-endurace-cf-7) | M | 177 to 184 cm |
| [Rose Backroad AL](/models/rose-backroad-al-apex) | M | 174 to 179 cm |
| [Cannondale CAAD14](/models/cannondale-caad14-1) | 54 or 56 | 170 to 180 / 178 to 185 cm |

At Trek an M runs from 162 to 173 cm, at Canyon from 177 to 184 cm. Someone who rides an M Canyon and buys an M Trek gets a bike that's a size too small.

## Letters and numbers

- **Letters** (XS to XL, at some brands 2XS or ML) say nothing about the frame itself, only where the brand puts that size.
- **Numbers** (48, 51, 54, 56, 58) are usually the length of the seat tube or a "virtual" seat tube in centimetres. A 56 is not the same at every brand either: one measures to the top of the tube, another to the middle of the top tube.
- **Two sizes for one height**, like 54 and 56 at 1.78 m, mean you're between sizes. The smaller one is more nimble and stretched-out low, the bigger one more upright and calm.

## Stack and reach say more

If the brand publishes geometry, look at **stack** (how high the handlebar sits) and **reach** (how far forward). They compare across brands. An M Domane has a stack of 575 mm and reach of 374 mm; an M Endurace 590 mm and 378 mm: the Canyon M is higher and a little longer, as you'd expect from its rider heights.

## What to do with an ad

1. **Find the size in the ad.** Often in the title or a photo of the frame sticker. No size? Ask for it, and for the seller's height.
2. **Find the model year.** Brands sometimes change their sizing between generations.
3. **Check it against that model's chart.** In the [size finder](/size) you pick the brand and model and fill in your height.
4. **Mind the rest.** A different stem or seatpost can change the fit a lot. Ask what was changed.
