An ad is the seller's story, not a spec sheet. Usually in good faith, but often not quite right. Bikecheck reads every ad you [paste](/) next to the brand's original spec sheet. These are the seven things we check, and that you can check yourself.

## 1. Which model is it exactly?

"Trek Domane" says little: our [catalogue](/models?q=domane) holds dozens of Domanes, from a €999 aluminium AL 2 to an SLR 9 over €10,000. Look in the text and photos for the full model, including the number (SL 5, SL 6) and the generation.

## 2. Is the year right?

This is the biggest trap. We went through a sample of **72 checked ads** and compared three things: the year in the text, the year that fits the colour in the photos, and the years that spec was sold. **Only 34 of the 72 agreed on all three.** In the rest at least one was off, usually because the seller gives the purchase year instead of the model year, or fills it in from memory.

![Each square is one ad from our sample.](chart:ad-years)

One year easily makes a 10% difference in value. When in doubt, ask for the receipt.

## 3. Does the groupset fit the model?

Each model comes with a set groupset. If the ad names a different one, something was replaced. An upgrade is fine (though hardly anyone pays extra for it second-hand); a cheaper groupset lowers the value. See also [what a groupset tells you about a bike](/blog/what-a-groupset-tells-you).

## 4. Does the size fit you?

A bargain in the wrong size is no bargain. Brands use different size systems (54, M, 56 cm). The [size finder](/size) shows which frame size fits your height, per model.

## 5. Is the price fair?

Put the asking price next to the new price of that model year and take off for age. The [value estimator](/value) does it for you. If the price is far below the fair range, read point 7.

## 6. What do the photos say?

Look at the colour (which year was it sold in?), the wheels, the tyres and the chain. A shiny bike with a worn chain and cassette is less "as good as new" than the text claims. Only photos from far away, or a catalogue photo? Ask for real close-ups.

## 7. Does the seller's story hold up?

A seller who knows little about the bike, has no receipt and won't meet at home is a warning sign. Read [how to spot a stolen bike](/blog/spotting-a-stolen-bike) before you pay.
