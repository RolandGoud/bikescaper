The Giant TCR is Giant's classic all-round road bike: light, stiff, and not expensive for carbon. In our sample of 72 checked ads, Giant was the second most common brand with 23, and the TCR is the biggest group among them.

## Advanced, Advanced Pro, Advanced SL

Giant puts its carbon grades in the name:

- **TCR Advanced** is the entry carbon. Now €2,799 (Advanced 2, 105) to €3,999 (Advanced 0, 105 Di2).
- **TCR Advanced Pro** has a lighter frame and often carbon wheels. Now €4,899 to €7,999.
- **TCR Advanced SL** is the lightest top frame. Now €8,399 to €12,499.

The number after it is the build: the lower, the better the parts. So an **Advanced 0** is better equipped than an **Advanced 2**.

**PC** or **Pro Compact** in the name means a compact crankset (smaller chainrings), easier in the hills. The frame is the same.

![](chart:tcr-rises)

A TCR Advanced 2 got nearly half again as expensive in four years. For a buyer that's good news: an older TCR is relatively cheap second-hand, *if* the seller works from the old new price.

**Worked example.** A 2022 TCR Advanced 2 Disc (€2,549 new) in normal used condition is worth about **€1,170 to €1,430** after four years, according to our [value estimator](/value).

## What to check on a used TCR

- **Rim or disc brakes?** Older TCRs without "Disc" have rim brakes. Fine bikes, but less sought after, and that shows in the price.
- **The size**: Giant uses S, M, ML, L. Check which fits you in the [size finder](/size).
- **The groupset**: a 2021 "Advanced 1" had a different groupset from today's. Paste the ad into the [check](/) and we put the parts next to that year's original.
