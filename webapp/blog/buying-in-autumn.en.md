In spring everyone wants a road bike at once. Autumn is different: the season is over, the new model years are in the shops, and riders who upgrade put their old bike up for sale. For a buyer that's a good time to look calmly.

## Why autumn works in your favour

- **Less competition.** If you look now, you less often have to reply within the hour to stand a chance.
- **Upgraders sell.** Anyone who already has next season's bike wants the old one gone before winter.
- **The seller can show you the bike.** A bike that has just ridden the season is easy to judge: it hasn't been standing in a shed for months.


One caveat: we don't (yet) measure how asking prices move per season, so we can't say how big that advantage is in euros. What is certain: the more time you have, the less you need to accept a price that's too high.

## Mind the model year

In autumn, brands launch their new model year. A bike bought new in October 2026 may already be a 2027 model, or a 2026 one from the sale. Sellers often give the year they bought the bike. For the value, the **model year** counts, and that's often a year earlier than the seller thinks. Read more in [reading a bike ad](/blog/reading-a-bike-ad).

## How to search in autumn

1. **Know your size** with the [size finder](/size), so you can rule ads out straight away.
2. **Know what you want to pay.** Look up a few models in the [catalogue](/models) and work out with the [value estimator](/value) what they're worth at their age.
3. **Check every ad** in the [check](/) before you reply: do the model, year, groupset and price add up?
4. **Test ride in the cold.** Pay extra attention to brakes and shifting: grease gets sticky at low temperatures, and a bike that still runs smoothly then has been well looked after.
5. **Ask about maintenance.** Chain, cassette, tyres and brake pads wear a lot in a summer. A seller who has just replaced them deserves a slightly higher price.
