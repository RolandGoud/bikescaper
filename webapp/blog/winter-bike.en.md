Anyone who keeps training through winter sees their bike suffer: road salt, mud, rain. A chain and cassette can be worn out in a few months, and an expensive carbon bike with an electronic groupset is something you'd rather not expose to that. So many riders buy a second bike for winter. Second-hand, and not too dear.

## What makes a good winter bike?

- **Aluminium or steel.** A scratch or a fall matters less than on carbon, and the bike is cheaper.
- **Disc brakes.** They keep braking in the rain and don't grind your rims away with road grit.
- **Room for wide tyres and mudguards.** 32 mm or wider gives grip and comfort; mudguards keep you and your drivetrain cleaner. Check the frame has mounts.
- **A simple mechanical groupset.** Claris, Sora, Tiagra or CUES: cheap to replace once the salt has done its work.

An endurance or gravel bike is often better for this than a pure race bike: they already have the room and the mounts.

## What to check on a used winter bike

A winter bike has often had a hard life already. Look extra closely at:

- **Rust** on the chain, bolts and brake rotors.
- **Chain and cassette wear.** A chain checker costs a few euros; if the chain is worn, the cassette often is too.
- **Brake pads and rotors.** Hydraulic brakes that feel spongy need bleeding.
- **Bearings.** Spin the wheels and the headset: grinding or play means replacing.
