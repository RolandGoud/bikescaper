Carbon is the default for a "real" road bike, and aluminium the budget option. But how big is the price gap really? We took every current road and gravel bike in our [catalogue](/models) with a price, a known frame material and a known groupset, and compared like with like: the same groupset, a different frame.

Of the bikes where we know the material, 754 are carbon, 122 aluminium and 1 is steel. Carbon dominates the catalogue, so the aluminium bikes are the ones to look for.

## Same groupset, different frame

The median new price per groupset:

| Groupset | Aluminium | Carbon | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shimano Tiagra | €1,399 (9 bikes) | €2,299 (5 bikes) | +€900 |
| Shimano 105 | €1,999 (14 bikes) | €2,699 (50 bikes) | +€700 |
| SRAM Apex | €2,499 (9 bikes) | €3,150 (10 bikes) | +€651 |
| Shimano GRX | €1,999 (28 bikes) | €3,499 (41 bikes) | +€1,500 |

GRX shows the biggest gap, but the carbon gravel bikes also more often have the more expensive GRX builds. The cleanest comparison is within one model family, where the brand puts exactly the same parts on both frames:

| Bike | Groupset | Aluminium | Carbon | Difference |

![Same groupset, aluminium and carbon frame: new price.](chart:carbon-premium)

So the carbon frame costs **€700 to €1,000** extra in most families. The last row is the exception: the Allez Sprint is Specialized's top aluminium race frame, and it costs more than an entry carbon Aethos with the same groupset. "Aluminium" is not automatically the cheap option.

## What you get back when you sell

A higher new price also means a higher second-hand price, but the gap shrinks with the years. Our [value estimator](/value) starts from the new price and takes off a share each year, so the difference in euros shrinks along with the price.

**Worked example.** After three years of normal use, a Domane AL 5 (€1,799 new) is worth about **€940 to €1,150**, and a Domane SL 5 (€2,499 new) about **€1,310 to €1,600**. Of the €700 extra you paid, you get back about €400.

## What to weigh up

- **Weight and ride.** A carbon frame is usually lighter and can be made more comfortable. For a fast group ride that matters, for commuting or winter riding much less.
- **Damage.** Aluminium dents and scratches, but a crack in carbon is harder to see and to judge. On a second-hand carbon bike, ask how it was stored and if it ever fell. Our [check](/) puts those questions next to the ad.
- **The parts.** For the same budget, aluminium often gets you a better groupset: a 105 aluminium bike costs about the same as a Tiagra carbon one. See [what a groupset tells you](/blog/what-a-groupset-tells-you) about the difference.
